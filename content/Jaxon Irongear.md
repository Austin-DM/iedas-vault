
Jaxon Irongear


Jaxon Irongear is a brilliant, secretive, and politically savvy human artificer and inventor, operating out of the industrial city of [[Beruda]], on the eastern edge of the continent of [[Iedas]]. Though publicly known as a master craftsman and magical engineer, his true role is far more ambitious: he is the founder and architect of [[Skyfleet]], a covert skyship project intended to revolutionize travel, warfare, and control over the continent.

⸻

Background

Jaxon Irongear was born in one of the more centralized regions of Iedas, but his early life remains largely undocumented. Unlike most artificers, Jaxon had no formal guild affiliation. Instead, he gained recognition through self-taught innovation and an innate magical gift that allowed him to perceive the hidden histories of people and objects.

He studied briefly in [[Mumperath]], in the continent’s foremost magical academy, but his time there was short—he found their structure too rigid and their pace too slow. After leaving, he befriended a [[Nell]], who offered him refuge and resources in the form of a [[Floating Cloud Castle]], where Jaxon continues to conduct high-level magical experiments away from public scrutiny.

⸻

The [[Skyfleet]] Project

Jaxon’s most important and secretive work is the development of [[Skyfleet]]: an elite fleet of magically powered airships and specialized operatives. The project is housed at the Sky Docks, a highly warded facility in a concealed area north of [[Beruda]]’s [[Grand Workshop]]

[[Skyfleet]] is more than a transportation system. It is a vision of strategic dominance—a way to unify fragmented territories under an aerial network no land-based power can match.

Only a handful of individuals know the full scope of [[Skyfleet]]. Even [[Beruda]]’s ruling council is kept in the dark, fed misinformation and decoys. Jaxon controls all access, backed by layers of magical wards, cover stories, and engineered public perceptions.

⸻

Magical Talent and Signature Ability

Jaxon is not just an artificer in name—he is a mage of singular vision, blessed at birth with the Sight of Echoed Essence, a [[Blessed children|blessing]] that allows him to see the residual magical history of objects and people.
	•	He does not simply see mana; he sees the trails, identities, and emotional echoes embedded in magical material.
	•	This enables him to identify foreign entities, such as individuals not native to [[Iedas]], by the incompatibility of their magical signatures.
	•	It also allows him to read the hidden properties of enchanted objects, trace enchantments, and understand the “life” of magical constructs.

Some speculate this sight is due to a blessing granted at birth, possibly by a primordial force or from exposure to ancient magical resonance.

Relationship to the [[party]]

Jaxon took immediate interest in the [[party]] when they arrived in [[Beruda]], recognizing their magical signatures as alien—never before seen on the continent. After a trial of cooperation (including a successful lumber delivery), he recruited them into [[Skyfleet]] under the codename [[Team Axel]], signifying their role as a central moving force in his project.

Role in the World

Jaxon straddles the line between visionary and manipulator. He seeks progress but understands that innovation in [[Iedas]] is dangerous—new power invites disruption, and the world is filled with factions that would steal, suppress, or destroy his work.

He is not evil. Nor is he good in a conventional sense. His loyalty lies with his vision, and he believes the ends justify his means.

He is:
	•	Pragmatic: Willing to lie, bribe, or mislead to protect his work.
	•	Cautious: Keeps secrets even from his allies.
	•	Brilliant: Capable of synthesizing magic and technology at a level few can approach.
	•	Charismatic: Despite his secrecy, he wins trust through sincerity and generosity.

Misconceptions

Despite being human, many people mistake Jaxon Irongear for a dwarf. This is due to a few overlapping factors:
	•	His surname, Irongear, is stereotypically dwarven in tone and structure.
	•	His fashion sense—including heavy leather aprons, soot-streaked goggles, and tool-laden belts—mirrors that of traditional dwarven smiths.
	•	He often speaks in gruff, clipped tones, especially when deep in thought or frustrated with inefficiency.
	•	His workshop and public persona revolve around metal, stone, and steam, reinforcing the image of a dwarven tinker.

Jaxon neither confirms nor denies these rumors. In fact, he finds the misunderstanding useful—it helps shield his more secretive activities, as outsiders assume he’s part of some reclusive dwarven enclave, rather than a lone human operating at the highest levels of magical innovation.
